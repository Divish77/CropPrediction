Three decades of harvest

Average yield across every tracked crop and country has climbed steadily since 1990 — better seed varieties, fertiliser and irrigation outpacing any climate drag, at least so far.

Reading this chart: the line is a simple mean across all 101 countries and 10 crops each year, so it hides enormous variation — a Wheat farm in Denmark and a Cassava plot in Chad both count once. The sections below break that variation apart.

Not all crops are equal

Yield is measured in tonnes per hectare, and starch-heavy root crops simply weigh more per plant than grains — this is a unit-of-measurement effect, not a "better crop."

The uncomfortable finding

Which crop you're growing and where explain roughly four-fifths of the variation in yield. Rainfall, temperature and pesticide use together explain the rest — real, but secondary to the structural question of what and where.

That doesn't mean climate doesn't matter. It means its effect shows up gradually, shifting the odds within a crop and country over years — which is exactly what the scatter plots below start to reveal.

Choosing a model

Trained on 1990–2009, tested against 2010–2013 — years the model never saw — the honest way to check if it generalises.

R² of 0.95 means the Random Forest explains 95% of yield variance on years it never trained on — the linear models cap out near 0.72, confirming the relationship is non-linear and interaction-heavy.
